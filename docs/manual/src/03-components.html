Components Reference

Every field, chip and switch on each component's card and Add dialog: what it means, which end or face it measures from, when it appears, and what it puts on the printed sheet. Written for the person building the shaft; the shop callouts say how each feature reads on paper.

How to use this reference

A shaft is built from five kinds of component: tapers, threads, liners, bodies and coupler bolt slots / holes. Keyways live on tapers and bodies. You add components from + Add Component and edit them on their cards in the carousel under the preview; that workflow, along with undo, overlaps and bare shaft, is covered in The Shaft Editor. This chapter is the field-by-field detail.

Conventions used in the tables

  • Units in labels. Every length and diameter field ends with the document unit, for example Length (in) or Length (mm). The tables leave the unit off.
  • Positions. All positions run along the shaft from the AFT end toward FWD unless a Measure From chip says otherwise.
  • Fractions. Every numeric field accepts decimals or shop fractions such as “3 1/2”, “3-1/2” or “11/16”. A decimal comma works too: “12,75” is 12.75. “1,500” is refused, because it could be 1.5 or 1500.
  • Units. A length or diameter may carry its unit: “260mm” on an inch drawing is 260 millimeters, and “11"” or “11in” on a millimeter drawing is 11 inches. The value then shows in the drawing's unit. Any other letters after a number (“12ft”, “4tpi”) are refused rather than ignored.
  • Refused values say so. In an Add dialog a field that cannot read its text says Not a number under it, and Add stays greyed until it is fixed. On a card the field puts the previous value back and names what it did not apply.
  • Values are kept as typed. If two components disagree, the app warns you; it does not move either one (see What you type is what prints).
  • Card vs. dialog. Everything that changes the shape, size or position of a component appears both on its card and in its Add dialog, under the same conditions. A handful of switches that only change how an already-drawn component prints are on the card only; see Per-component drawing switches.

Tapers

A taper is a conical section, usually the propeller taper at the AFT end or the coupling taper at the FWD end. The app works in shop terms: S.E.T. (small end of taper) and L.E.T. (large end of taper). The small end always faces the nearer end of the shaft. A taper whose middle lies in the aft half of the shaft has its S.E.T. at its aft end; one in the forward half has its S.E.T. at its forward end.

How the rate is worked out

  • Auto needs Length, S.E.T. Ø and L.E.T. Ø. If the result is within 3% of a common rate (1:20, 1:16, 1:14, 1:12, 1:10 or 1:8), it shows that rate. Otherwise it shows the exact figure to three decimals, such as “1:15.875”. When two common rates are equally close, 1:16 wins on bores of 6″ and under and 1:12 above.
  • With only one diameter filled in, Auto shows Auto needs Length + SET + LET. Switch to Manual to derive the missing end.
  • Manual keeps exactly what you type. If the typed rate disagrees with the three dimensions by more than 3%, the card shows Rate does not match Length + SET + LET. Nothing is changed; you decide which number is wrong.
  • A plain whole number is read as 1:N, so “16” means 1:16. A bare “1” is refused as ambiguous.

Type rates as a ratio (“1:16”) or as inches per foot with the “/ft” written out (“3/4″/ft”). A bare fraction such as “3/4” is refused — it could mean 3/4″ per foot or a diameter change of 0.75 per unit of length, a very steep taper — and the card says Write 3/4"/ft or a ratio like 1:16; a bare fraction is ambiguous. The same goes for “.75” typed as a decimal: it is 3/4 without the “/ft”, and the card asks for .75"/ft or a ratio. A decimal slope of 0.25 or less, such as .0625 (3/4″/ft), is still read as a slope. The shop notation 3/4″/ft is 1:16; 1″/ft is 1:12.

Adding a taper from S.E.T. and rate

  1. Tap + Add Component, then Taper.
  2. Choose Measure From: AFT or FWD and enter the start and Length.
  3. Enter S.E.T. Ø. Leave L.E.T. Ø blank.
  4. Tap Manual and enter the rate, for example “1:12” (the dialog starts with “1:12”).
  5. Tap Add. The app works out the L.E.T. from the length and rate.

For working a taper out from other combinations of numbers, use the taper calculator (see Calculators and tools).

What prints for a taper

  • The taper outline at its true diameters. A taper may be drawn shorter than scale on a long shaft, but two tapers always keep their true length ratio.
  • A length dimension above the shaft for the end tapers.
  • A block in the footer column for its half of the shaft: “Rate:”, “L.E.T.:”, “S.E.T.:”, “Length:”, then the keyway lines if it has one. The block is headed with the taper's name if you renamed it; otherwise “AFT Taper” or “FWD Taper” for the taper at that end of the shaft, and plain “Taper” for one part-way along (a step-down taper is never called the FWD taper).
  • Its name below the shaft, unless names are off (see Component names).

On inch sheets a 1:12 rate prints as “1″/ft” and 1:16 as “3/4″/ft”. Other rates print as typed. The footer L.E.T. and S.E.T. carry no AFT/FWD suffix; the column heading says which end of the shaft you are looking at.

Threads

A thread is an external threaded section: the propeller-nut thread beyond the S.E.T., or a coupling-nut thread. It draws as a hatched section whose hatch leans with the pitch.

Imperial or metric

The Add Thread dialog offers two chips: Imperial (TPI) and Metric (mm). The choice is made when the thread is added and decides which fields the card shows from then on.

  • Imperial: Major Ø in the document unit and TPI.
  • Metric: one Thread designation field, such as “M20×2.5”. You may type “x”, “X” or “*” for the ×, and a decimal comma as DIN and ISO prints write it (“M20x2,5”). A designation the field cannot read says Not a metric designation — e.g. M20×2.5. The designation carries both the major Ø and the pitch, so no other diameter field is shown. A metric thread prints in millimeters even on an inch drawing.

A coarse designation with no pitch, such as “M20”, is accepted and prints as typed, but the thread then has no pitch and the card warns Zero pitch — thread renders flat. Include the pitch.

There is no Imperial/Metric switch on the card. To change a thread from TPI (threads per inch) to a metric designation, or back, remove it and add it again.

Threads excluded from the OAL

A typical propeller shaft is quoted S.E.T. to S.E.T., with the nut threads standing outside that figure. Turn Include thread in OAL off and pick the Thread end. The app places the thread flush against that end of the shaft, outside the dimensioned length, and keeps it there when the OAL changes. The OAL dimension on the sheet stops at the shaft end and the thread prints beyond it.

Thread relief

A relief is the plain groove turned below the thread root where the thread runs up to its shoulder, so the tool can run out and the nut seats. Turn on Thread relief and three more controls appear:

Field / controlWhat it means
Relief lengthAxial length of the groove. Starts at 1 1/2 pitches (3/8″ on a 4 TPI thread); type over it.
Relief ØDiameter of the groove. Starts just under the thread root (major Ø less 1.3 pitches); type over it.
Relief at: AFT | FWDWhich end of the thread carries the groove. The app first suggests the inboard end: FWD for a thread at the AFT end of the shaft, AFT for one at the FWD end. The groove follows the thread: in the Add dialog until you tap these chips, and on the card when Thread end or Include thread in OAL moves the thread to the other end (the same Undo step). A groove you put at the other end yourself stays put.
  • The relief is part of the thread's length. Turning it on moves nothing: the thread keeps its start and length, and the last Relief length of it becomes groove instead of thread.
  • Turning the switch off removes the relief. Turning it back on, while the card is still open, restores the values you had.
  • Leaving a relief field blank does not remove the relief; only the switch does.
  • The card warns Relief Ø must be below the thread Ø or Relief longer than the thread when the numbers cannot work. The typed numbers are kept; only the drawing is limited.

What prints for a thread

  • The hatched thread section, with the relief groove drawn at its own diameter.
  • A footer line for a thread at either end of the shaft: “Thread: 5″ × 4 TPI × 6″” (major Ø × pitch × length), or “Thread: M20×2.5 × <length>” for a metric thread, with the designation printed as typed.
  • Directly under it, for a relieved thread: “Thread relief: <length> × Ø <diameter>”.
  • No separate dimension rail. A thread counted in the OAL sits inside the OAL dimension.

The thread length on the footer is the whole threaded section, groove included. The relief line gives the groove length and diameter to turn. The wear document draws the thread as a plain hatched section with no relief.

Liners

A liner is a sleeve shrunk or bonded over the shaft, such as a bearing or seal liner. It has a length and an outer diameter; the shaft underneath carries on as a body.

Liner shoulders

A shoulder is a machined step at a liner end: over the outermost part of the liner the OD drops to a reduced diameter, with a radiused corner where the step meets the full OD. The step is cut into the liner itself; nothing else on the shaft moves.

The shoulder controls are hidden until you switch them on at Settings → Drawing → Liner shoulders, because most shafts never need them. A liner that already has shoulder values always shows its shoulder controls, whatever that setting says.

Field / controlWhat it means
AFT and FWD rows: None | ShoulderWhether that end of the liner is stepped.
Shoulder lengthHow far in from the liner end the reduced diameter runs.
Shoulder ØThe reduced diameter.
Edge radius: buttonThe corner radius where the step face meets the liner OD, picked from a list: Sharp, 1/16″, 1/8″, 3/16″, 1/4″, 3/8″, 1/2″ (shown in mm on a metric drawing). Starts at Sharp.

Choosing None on an end clears that end's shoulder length, Ø and radius. Undo brings them back.

What prints for a liner

  • The liner outline over the shaft, with any shoulders stepped in.
  • Two dimensions above the shaft: the distance from the nearer S.E.T. to the liner's near edge (left out when the liner sits right on the S.E.T.), and the liner length. Which S.E.T. is used follows Settings → PDF Export Options → Measurement reference, not the liner's own Measure From chip.
  • An OD callout below the shaft (on by default; see Show Ø on drawing).
  • For a shoulder with a radius: one footer line per liner, “Liner shoulder R: <radius>” (“AFT r, FWD r” when the two ends differ). A sharp corner prints nothing.

On a long shaft a liner may be drawn shorter than scale so the sheet fits the page; the dimension still gives the true length. The shoulder step is drawn to scale but its length and reduced Ø are not dimensioned on the sheet; only the edge radius prints, in the footer.

Bodies

Bodies are the plain cylindrical shaft between the other components. The app fills every uncovered gap with bare shaft (an auto-body) on its own; you add an explicit body when a section needs its own diameter, a keyway, or a name. How the two kinds work together, and how to promote or demote one, is in The Shaft Editor.

A body may run under a liner or up against a taper, and a body with a keyway is never split; see Bodies and bare shaft.

Bare-shaft card

A bare-shaft card is titled Body (auto). Start and Length are greyed out because the surrounding components decide them. Diameter is editable: with more than one stretch of bare shaft it asks whether the new diameter is for This section or All auto sections (see Bodies and bare shaft). The card also carries Show bare-shaft Ø on drawing, the same Face finish and Seal areas controls as an explicit body, and the Explicit body checkbox that promotes it.

Blends and seal areas set on an auto section belong to that stretch of bare shaft and follow it when the components around it move. Promoting the section starts the new explicit body with square faces and no seal areas; set them again on its card.

Face finish: Square or Blend

A blend is a smooth machined transition at a body end, curving from the diameter on the other side of the face into the body's own diameter, with no square shoulder and no stated taper rate. The curve is cut from the body that carries it; no other component changes.

Field / controlWhat it means
AFT row: Square | BlendFinish of the body's AFT face.
Blend AFTShown while AFT is Blend: how far in from the face the curve runs. Starts at 2″ (or a quarter of a short body).
FWD row and Blend FWDThe same for the FWD face.
Shape: S-curve | Fillet | Eased coneShown once either face is blended; one shape for both faces. S-curve is smooth at both ends (default). Fillet is smooth at the large end and meets the small end at a corner. Eased cone is a straight cone with both corners softened.
  • The blend takes its starting diameter from whatever is across the face. If there is nothing there, or the neighbor has the same Ø, no blend is drawn.
  • Where a liner butts the face, as at a seal area, the curve starts from halfway between the liner OD and the body Ø. The seat under a liner is never drawn, so this is a visual cue only.
  • Switching a face back to Square sets its blend length to zero. The seal areas are not touched.

Seal areas

A seal area is the stretch at a body end that the fiberglass seats into, cut with small radius grooves. It belongs to the body section, not to the face finish: a body can have grooves at a square end, grooves behind a blended end, or a blend with no grooves.

Field / controlWhat it means
AFT, FWD checkboxesWhether that end has a seal area.
Seal area AFT, Seal area FWDShown under a ticked end: the length of the seal area, measured in from the face. Starts at 4″ (or a quarter of a short body).
  • The seal area is always measured from the face, so it sits in the same place whether the face is square or blended.
  • On an explicit body, unticking a seal area stops drawing it but keeps the typed length; ticking it again brings it back.

What prints for a body

  • The body outline, at its own diameter, with any blends curved in.
  • Seal areas as three evenly spaced dashed lines across the body, outline to outline. The silhouette stays flat through the seal area.
  • Every explicit body's Ø in the footer “Body:” line, whatever its Ø callout switch says.
  • A Ø callout below the shaft only if Show Ø on drawing is on (off by default).
  • No dimension rail for a body, a blend or a seal area. The rails always dimension the stored body ends as if they were square.

The three dashed lines are an indication that the seal area is grooved, not a count to machine: cut the number of grooves your seat calls for. They are dashed so they are not mistaken for a component face, which is a solid line. A short blend is drawn wider than scale so it can be seen at all; it carries no number, so nothing on the sheet is exaggerated that you would machine from. The wear document draws body ends square.

Keyways (bodies and tapers)

Tapers and explicit bodies can each carry one keyway. The controls are the same on both, with two differences: a body keyway chooses which end it runs from, and a taper keyway always runs from the S.E.T.

The Keyway checkbox

The whole keyway section, including its unit chip, sizes, captured and spooned switches and the clocking switches, sits behind one Keyway checkbox.

  • On a card, the checkbox opens on its own whenever any keyway value (W, D or L) is filled in, so a half-typed keyway stays visible.
  • Ticking it only shows the fields. Nothing is stored until you type a value.
  • Unticking it on a card that holds keyway values asks Remove keyway? (“This clears the keyway's width, depth, length and inset from this component.”) Choose Remove or Cancel. This is the only way to close a section that holds values; if you undo the removal, the section reopens with the values back.
  • In an Add dialog the checkbox starts off, and the dialog adds no keyway while it is off, even if you typed into the fields before unticking.

Keyway values show as shop fractions down to 1/32″ on inch drawings.

Where a keyway sits

  • Taper: an open keyway starts at the S.E.T. and runs toward the L.E.T. for its length. Its closed end, where a spoon goes, is toward the L.E.T.
  • Body: an open keyway starts at the face chosen under KW from and runs into the body. Its closed end is away from that face.
  • Captured: the same, but starting the inset distance in from the S.E.T. or face, with both ends closed.

A new body keyway's KW from chip starts on the end opposite the shaft's existing keyway when there is exactly one, so a second keyway on a shaft with an AFT taper keyway starts at FWD. Otherwise it starts at AFT. This is a starting choice only; tap the other chip to change it.

Turning Captured keyway off sets the inset to zero, which is what makes the keyway open. Turning it back on while the card is open restores the inset you had. Typing a blank or zero inset does not un-capture the keyway; only the switch does.

Keyway clocking

When the shaft carries two or more keyways, each keyway's section shows two switches:

ControlWhat it means
Keyways 180° apartThe keyways are on opposite sides of the shaft.
Keyways 90° apartThe keyways are a quarter turn apart. Turning one of these on turns the other off.
From AFT keyway, viewed from aft: CW | CCWShown with 90°: which way the other keyway lies from the AFT keyway, looking from aft.
  • These are settings for the whole shaft. Changing them on one card changes them for every keyway.
  • A keyway counts once it has a width, depth and length. In an Add dialog the switches appear when the shaft already has a keyway and the dialog's own keyway is fully filled in.
  • The keyway nearest the AFT end is the reference keyway; the others are drawn relative to it.

What prints for a keyway

  • The slot in plan view on the shaft, at its true width relative to the drawn shaft. A body keyway and the stretch around it are never shortened on the drawing, even when the rest of the body is.
  • Clocking: at 180° the other keyways draw as dashed hidden slots. At 90° they draw as a notch in the shaft outline: on the bottom edge for CW (clockwise), the top edge for CCW (counter-clockwise). A footer note reads “Keyways 180° apart”, “Keyways 90° apart (CW from aft)” or “Keyways 90° apart (CCW from aft)”.
  • Footer size line in that end's column — KW (keyway) is the footer's shorthand: “KW: W × D × L” for a taper, “Body KW: W × D × L” for a body, in the keyway's own unit. When that column also holds a taper's block, the body line carries the body's name (“Body #1 KW: …”), so it can't be read as the taper's keyway.
  • Captured: a line below it, “Captured KW: inset <distance> from S.E.T.” (taper) or “… from AFT” / “… from FWD” (body). The slot draws closed at both ends.
  • Spooned: “(spooned)” after the size, and a note “KW length to base of spoon (mill end)”. The slot draws normally, with an enlarged spoon around its closed end; the mill end stays visible inside the spoon.

The footer is the keyway's specification; the slot on the drawing shows where it is. The KW length on a spooned keyway is to the base of the spoon, where the mill cut ends, not to the tip of the spoon. A captured keyway never prints as spooned.

Coupler bolt slots and cross-drilled holes

This component is one axial row of bolt holes at a coupling. Add it with Coupler Bolt Slot / Hole in the Add Component chooser. It comes in two styles:

Seam
The muff-coupler cutout: a radial hole in the seam between shaft and coupling sleeve, half in the shaft and half in the sleeve. The app draws only the shaft side. This is the default.
Cross-drilled
A bolt hole drilled straight through the shaft on a diameter, at a plain coupling end. It is located from the end of the shaft to the hole center.

Bolt slots and holes are reference marks (see Reference marks never change the shaft): you can place them over a body, a liner or anything else.

What prints for a bolt slot or hole

  • Seam: a circle at each hole position, straddling the shaft outline top and bottom. A seam row prints no footer line; the only number it can print is the rail dimension its Show dimension rail switch turns on.
  • Either style: the row is never shortened on paper. Its holes keep their true spacing even inside a body that is compressed around them, and the body's S-break is placed clear of the row.
  • Cross-drilled, 90° from keyway: the bore drawn as hidden lines, two dashed walls one hole width apart running down from the top of the shaft, all the way through or, for a blind hole, to the drill depth with a dashed bottom.
  • Cross-drilled, in line with keyway: one circle on the shaft centerline at the hole position.
  • Cross-drilled footer lines, in the column of the end it was measured from: “Bolt hole: Ø <dia>” (with “× <count>” for more than one), “Hole center from FWD:” or “Hole center from AFT:” with the distance (and “@ <spacing>” for more than one), and, when the shaft has a keyway, “90° from keyway” or “In line with keyway”.

The hole-center distance in the footer is measured from the shaft end named on the line to the center of the hole. On the runout sheet's coupling end view, the bolt count comes from the first seam row; a cross-drilled hole is not a flange bolt and is not counted there.

Show Ø on drawing

Controls the diameter callout that hangs below the shaft for this component. The sheet prints one callout per distinct diameter, placed on the longest shown component with that diameter, so hiding one body moves the callout to another body of the same Ø rather than removing it. Body and liner callouts are separate; a liner OD is never merged with a body Ø.

Reach for it when a callout lands somewhere the diameter could not have been measured, such as a body running under fiberglass. Model the covered run and the bare window as separate bodies and show the Ø only on the window. The footer “Body:” line still lists every body diameter.

On a bare-shaft card, Show bare-shaft Ø on drawing is one switch for all bare shaft at once.

Shade on drawing

Grey shading is set by kind at Settings → PDF Export Options → Shade in Components (Bodies, Tapers, Liners; tapers and liners on, bodies off by default), and repeated on each document's PDF options. The card switch works in three ways:

  • Untouched: the switch shows, and follows, the Settings checkbox for that kind.
  • Switched on: this component shades even with its kind off. Use it to grey out one named section and leave the rest of the drawing clean.
  • Switched off: this component stays bare even with its kind on.

Once you flip the switch, that component keeps your choice even if the Settings checkbox changes later. The preview box fills the components that will print shaded and leaves the others empty.

On a consolidated sheet that prints measured diameters inside the profile, liners always print unshaded, whatever this switch says, so the values stay readable.

Show name on drawing

Controls whether this component's name prints below the shaft. It works the same three ways as shading: untouched, it follows Settings → Drawing → Component names on drawing (on by default), and the line under the switch reads Following Settings → Drawing → Component names; switched on, the name prints even with that setting off; switched off, it is hidden even with the setting on. Hiding one name never renumbers the others. Bare shaft never prints a name.

Compress on drawing

On a long shaft the sheet shortens plain body runs so everything fits the page, and draws an S-break in a run that is shortened a lot. Compress on drawing decides whether this explicit body may be shortened.

  • Off (the default for every body you add or promote): the body draws at true length and never carries an S-break. A named section reads at its real proportion. The drawn shaft height gives way, if it has to, to keep it true.
  • On: the body may be shortened and broken like bare shaft. Turn it on for a very long explicit body that would otherwise squeeze the rest of the drawing flat.

Bodies in older saved jobs may have it on. When an S-break appears is set at Settings → Drawing → Body S-break; how compression works is in Shaft height and compression, and what the break means on paper in S-breaks.

Prints in: in | mm

Prints this one component's values in a different unit from the document. It appears at the foot of the card only when Settings → Drawing → Per-component units is on. It changes how the component prints, not how you type it: the card's fields stay in the document unit. Tapping the document's own unit clears the choice.

A keyway has its own Keyway in: chip, which does change the unit you type the keyway in, because a metric keyway on an inch shaft should be entered in millimeters. That chip is in the Add dialogs as well as on the cards. A metric thread prints in millimeters without any chip.

Show dimension rail

On an explicit body's card, Show dimension rail prints the body's own length as a dimension above the shaft, on the schematic and the consolidated sheet. It works the same three ways as shading:

  • Untouched: it follows where the body sits. A body that runs to either end of the shaft, such as the coupling body at the FWD end, prints its length, and the line under the switch reads On — body is at a shaft end. A body in the middle of the shaft does not, and the line reads Off — mid-shaft body.
  • Switched on: the length prints wherever the body sits.
  • Switched off: the length is not printed, even for an end body.

The dimension runs over the whole body you entered, even where an S-break shortens it on the sheet. When another dimension on the sheet already spans exactly the same stretch (for example a liner's dimension from the shaft end to a liner that butts the end body), the length is printed once, not twice. A body that runs the full length of the shaft adds nothing, since the OAL is its length. Bare shaft never prints one.

On coupler bolt slot and hole cards, Show dimension rail is off until you switch it on. Off, the row prints no dimension; on, the schematic and consolidated sheets add one dimension rail from the end of the shaft the row is measured from (Measure From) to the center of the nearest hole, carrying the distance you entered. It changes nothing about the row itself, which is why it is not in the Add dialog. See Coupler bolt holes.

Component names

Every taper, thread, liner and explicit body has a name. Until you give it one, the app names it by position:

KindDefault names
TaperAFT Taper or FWD Taper, by which end is smaller; numbered (AFT Taper #2) only when two share a name.
ThreadAFT Thread or FWD Thread, by which half of the shaft it is in; numbered when two share a name.
LinerAFT Liner, MID Liner or FWD Liner, by position along the shaft; with exactly two liners they are always AFT and FWD. Numbered when two share a name.
Explicit bodyBody #1, Body #2… from AFT to FWD.
Bare shaftBody (auto); cannot be renamed.
Bolt slot / holeCoupler Bolt Slot or Coupler Bolt Hole, by style; cannot be renamed.

Renaming a component

  1. Tap the card title, or the pencil beside it.
  2. Type the name, for example “SKF bearing seat”.
  3. Press Done, or tap elsewhere on the card.

To go back to the default name, clear the field and press Done. A renamed component keeps its name wherever it moves.

How names print

  • Names print below the shaft, centered under their component.
  • Names share the space below the shaft with the Ø callouts. If a name would collide with a callout or another name, it first slides along its own component, then drops to another row only if it must. Callouts never move to make room.
  • On a crowded sheet the names shrink a point at a time, down to 7 pt. A name is never left off.
  • Whether a name prints at all is set by Show name on drawing and the Component names on drawing setting; see above. Template mode prints no names.

A short name reads best. A name much wider than its component may slide off-center to stay clear of the callouts.
