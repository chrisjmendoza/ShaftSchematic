Recording Shop Measurements

How to type runout readings, wear marks, measured diameters, undercut sections and worn sections into a job on a tablet or phone at the lathe, and how to work from blank paper sheets when the tablet stays in the office. This chapter is written for the machinist and foreman doing the measuring.

Before you start

Open the job and find the right tab

Every measurement belongs to a job that already has its shaft drawn. Open the job from the home screen (see The home screen), then open the navigation menu (the menu button at the top left; always visible on a wide tablet) and pick the tab you need:

TabWhat you record there
Runout SheetTIR readings and high-spot clock positions, one per bubble; the coupling pilot runout; station counts and bubble positions.
Wear DocumentWear spots on liners, pit and dye-failure X marks, measured diameters, the dye pen PASS/FAIL result.
Undercut DrawingUndercut sections: distance, length and measured Ø of each machined-below-surface span.
Consolidated OutputWorn sections: measured areas whose Ø values print inside the shaft on the consolidated sheet.

These tabs stay greyed out until the shaft is built — it needs at least one component and an overall length. If a tab won't open, the office has not finished the drawing yet.

Every one of these tabs has a ? button in its toolbar that opens the in-app Help at that tab's own guide.

Readings never change the shaft

Everything in this chapter is a reference mark laid over the drawing (see Reference marks never change the shaft). You cannot damage the drawing by recording measurements, and the office cannot lose your readings by tidying up the drawing.

Readings are saved with the job

Readings live inside the job file, next to the shaft. While you have unsaved work the job name at the top of the tab shows an asterisk (*). Tap the Save icon in the tab's toolbar to save; a job that has never been saved asks for a name first.

Readings stay where you took them

A runout reading belongs to its bubble — this component, this station number — not to a spot on the paper. If the office later edits the shaft, the reading follows its bubble. If an edit removes the station altogether (the component is deleted, or its station count is lowered), the reading is simply not drawn; it is not thrown away, and it reappears if the station comes back. Pits and measured diameters behave the same way with their component.

Wear spots are the one exception: they belong to their liner, and if that liner is deleted from the shaft its wear spots are dropped the next time the job is opened.

Undo

Typed readings, bubble moves, station count changes, wear marks, undercuts and worn sections all go into the job's single undo history. To undo, switch to the Schematic tab, tap the History icon in its toolbar and choose Undo (or Redo); see Undo and redo. The Runout Sheet tab also has its own one-tap Undo move for the last bubble you dragged — see Moving bubbles.

Units and typing values

  • Every entry field takes the job's unit (inches or millimeters), shown in the field label — for example Length (in).
  • Wear, undercut and worn-section fields accept decimals or fractions: 5.748, 3 1/2 and 7/8 all work.
  • Type TIR readings as decimals (.002). The bubble prints thousandths with the leading zero dropped, the way a TIR is hand-written: .010, .002.
  • What you type is stored exactly as typed. A value that looks wrong is shown with a warning, never changed.

Runout

The Runout Sheet tab

From top to bottom on a phone:

  1. The preview — the shaft with every bubble drawn below it, pinned at the top so it stays on screen while you scroll. Pinch to zoom; the Reset view button (circular arrow) in its top-right corner returns to the full shaft. Beside it is the Move bubbles pencil toggle.
  2. A one-line hint under the preview: Tap a bubble to enter its TIR reading and high spot. Press and hold one to drag it along its component.
  3. TIR orientation — which way the indicator was read.
  4. Coupling face and Pilot runout…
  5. Blank draft (write-in) and the Print Runout Sheet, Preview Runout Sheet and Export Runout Sheet PDF buttons.
  6. Measurement stations — the station count for each component, at the bottom because it is only needed when the sheet needs adjusting.

Stations and bubbles

A station is one indicator position on a component. Its bubble is the circle hanging below the shaft where the reading is written; the notch at the top of every bubble is the keyway, so the clock position of the high spot reads from the key. Stations are laid out automatically, by component length:

ComponentDefault stationsPlacement
Body (including bare-shaft sections)One per 20″ of length, at least 1Spread evenly along the body as drawn
LinerOne per 20″ of length, at least 2The end stations are set in 1″ from each edge
TaperAlways 2Set in 1″ from each end
ThreadNoneThreads are not measured for runout

Stations on liners and tapers are set in from the edges because the geometry changes at an edge and an indicator reads unreliably there. The automatic count stops at 10 stations per component; you can go higher by hand.

A body that is split by liners or tapers still counts as one body: its stations are worked out once from its total length, shared across the pieces, and numbered straight through from AFT to FWD. A short leftover piece next to a liner gets no bubble of its own.

Enter a TIR reading and high spot

  1. Make sure the pencil toggle in the preview's corner is off (not tinted).
  2. Tap the bubble. A large copy of the bubble opens, titled with the component and station — for example Body 1 · Station 2.
  3. Type the reading into the TIR field in the middle of the circle.
  4. Tap the ring (or drag a finger around it) at the high spot. The red marker snaps to the nearest half hour, and the line under the circle confirms it, for example High spot: 4:30. The 12, 3, 6 and 9 positions are labeled; the keyway notch sits at 12.
  5. Tap Save.

The value and the high spot are independent: save a value with no high spot, or a high spot with no value. Until a high spot is set the dialog reads No high spot set. Only touches on the ring move the marker — the middle of the circle is the value field, and touches well outside the rim are ignored.

The reading appears in the bubble on the preview at once, with the high spot as a short red dash across the rim. Tap the bubble again at any time to change it.

Clear
Empties the value and removes the high spot in the dialog. Tap Save afterwards to remove the reading from the bubble.
Cancel
Closes the dialog and keeps whatever the bubble held before.
Save
Stores the reading. Saving with neither a value nor a high spot removes the reading.

Set the TIR orientation

Under TIR orientation, tap Looking AFT or Looking FORWARD for the direction you stood when reading the indicator; the chosen button is filled. The sheet prints the choice on its bottom line, “TIR's taken looking: AFT” or “TIR's taken looking: FORWARD”, so whoever reads the high-spot marks knows which way round they are. Not set (the default) prints a blank write-in line instead.

Change how many stations a component has

Scroll to Measurement stations at the bottom of the tab. Each body, taper and liner has a row, AFT to FWD, showing its current count between a − and a +. Bare-shaft sections are listed as Body (auto). The preview above updates as you tap.

  • + adds a station. On a component whose bubbles have not been moved by hand, the stations re-space themselves evenly.
  • − removes a station — on an untouched component, the FWD-most one. A reading on the removed station is kept out of sight and comes back if you tap + again.
  • Take a count down to 0 to stop measuring that component: it draws no bubbles at all. Any readings it held are kept and reappear when you raise the count.

The same rows appear on the Consolidated Output tab under Runout bubbles (tap the heading to expand them), so you can adjust counts while looking at the consolidated sheet. Both places change the same counts.

Moving bubbles

Sometimes a station has to sit where the indicator can actually reach — clear of a bearing, a flange or a pit. To move a bubble:

  1. Press and hold the bubble until the phone gives a short buzz and the bubble's ring turns heavy.
  2. Without lifting, slide it along the shaft. Its leader line follows the station across the component.
  3. Lift your finger. The new position is saved.

After a drag, an Undo move button appears beside the hint under the preview. One tap puts that bubble back exactly where it was before the drag — if it had never been moved, back to automatic placement.

The rules a moved bubble follows:

  • A moved bubble stays exactly where you put it. Nothing the app does later moves it — not a geometry change, not a sheet layout change.
  • Only the bubble you dragged is fixed. The other stations on that component keep their automatic spacing and keep adjusting if the shaft changes.
  • A bubble stays on its own component and in its own order. It cannot be dragged off the component, and it stops 1/2″ short of its neighbors — it can never pass one. Station numbers therefore never change under a typed reading.
  • A reading travels with its bubble when you move it.

A row in Measurement stations whose bubbles have been moved shows a Reset button in place of the word Stations:. Reset returns that component's bubbles to automatic placement; Reset all bubble positions under the rows does it for every component. Both can be undone from the History menu.

On a component with moved bubbles, + and − keep every bubble planted: + puts the new station in the widest gap (between the usual pair, on a two-station taper), and − removes the station that is most redundant and has no reading, so − undoes a +. Every reading stays on the bubble it was taken at.

Move mode

When you are arranging several bubbles, press-and-hold gets slow. Move mode turns the preview into an arranging surface:

  1. Tap the pencil toggle in the preview's top-right corner (Move bubbles). It fills with color while Move mode is on, and the hint under the preview changes.
  2. Touch any bubble and drag it straight away — no hold needed.
  3. Tap a bubble to select it, or tap a component on the shaft to select the whole component (it gets a dashed frame). Tapping bare paper clears the selection.
  4. Use the − and + buttons on the strip under the preview to remove or add a station on the selected component. The strip names the selection, for example Body 2 · Station 3, or Nothing selected.
  5. Tap the pencil toggle again (Done moving bubbles) to go back to entering readings.

In Move mode a tap never opens the reading dialog, and pinch-zoom still works from bare paper. The same movement rules apply as for a press-and-hold drag, and Undo move still appears after each drag. The − / + buttons are the same as the station rows; undo them from the History menu.

Coupling face and pilot runout

The sheet can carry the coupling end view the shops hand-sketch in the corner, with the pilot runout written inside the bore (see The runout sheet).

  1. Tick Coupling face on the Runout Sheet tab (End view drawn bottom-right, taken looking forward.).
  2. Tap Pilot runout… beside it (it is greyed out until the face is ticked).
  3. The bubble dialog opens titled Coupling pilot. Type the TIR and tap the ring at the high spot, exactly as for a station.
  4. Tap Save.

The coupling face is a per-job choice: one job may measure the coupling and the next may not. The same setting appears as a Coupling face chip on the runout preview's options and as a switch on the Consolidated Output tab — all three change the same thing. The number of bolt holes comes from the shaft's first coupler bolt slot; a shaft with none draws the plain two-circle face.

Print the runout sheet

The Runout Sheet tab produces the classic runout sheet: shaft outline, OAL, bubbles and readings, the TIR direction line and, if ticked, the coupling face.

  1. Tap Preview Runout Sheet to see the actual page.
  2. Tap Print Runout Sheet to send it to a printer, or Export Runout Sheet PDF to save a PDF file.

If a print button is greyed out, a red message above it explains what in the drawing needs fixing first. The consolidated sheet is printed from the Consolidated Output tab. The preview's options (shaft height, bubble size and height, and the rest) are covered in The PDF options panel; how to read each sheet is in Reading the Printed Sheets.

Wear

The Wear Document tab

The tab shows the shaft with every body, taper and liner tinted as a tap target. A small number badge above a component counts the wear spots and pits already recorded on it. Under the drawing the hint reads Tap a body, taper, or liner to inspect wear and mark pits. Below that are the Blank draft (write-in) switch and the Print Wear Document, Preview Wear Document and Export Wear Document PDF buttons, then the per-job options: Trace depth exaggeration, Dye pen inspection: and Components.

Open a component's wear inspection

  1. Tap the liner, taper or body on the drawing.
  2. A full-screen view opens, titled with the component's name and “— wear inspection” — for example Stern tube liner — wear inspection. It shows that component broken out of the shaft with short stubs of its neighbors, AFT at the left and FWD at the right (labeled ← AFT and FWD → under the drawing).
  3. Pinch with two fingers to zoom in (up to 6×) and pan for precise placement. A one-finger tap is always a placement tap.
  4. Tap the back arrow (Back to Wear Document) or use the system Back gesture when you're done.

What you can record depends on the component:

ComponentPits (X marks)Measured ØWear spots
LinerYesYes (also drives the worn-profile trace)Yes
TaperYesYesNo
Body or bare shaftYesYesNo

The view opens with the Add X tool selected, so the first tap on the drawing places a pit mark. To record a diameter instead, pick Add Ø first. A stray X is removed with Remove X.

Mark pits and dye-penetrant failures

The machinist's convention is an X on the sketch: a small X for a small pit or hole, a larger X for a bigger cavity. The size is a symbol, not a measurement of the pit.

  1. Under Pits, set Tool: to Add X.
  2. Pick Size: Small or Large. The size applies to the X marks you place next.
  3. Tap the component where the pit is. The X lands where you tap — along the length and up or down across the face — so you can show roughly where on the surface it sits.

To take one off, choose Remove X and tap the X; a tap that misses does nothing. Clear all pits removes every X on this component. The heading counts them, for example 3 placed.

Record measured diameters

  1. Under Diameter measurements, set Tool: to Add Ø.
  2. Tap the component at the station you measured.
  3. The Add Ø reading dialog opens and shows where you tapped, for example “At 4.25 in from the AFT edge”.
  4. Type the micrometer reading into Measured Ø (in).
  5. Tap Save.

The station draws as a thin tick across the component with the value below it on a leader line, exactly as it will print. Save stays greyed out until the field holds a value above zero, and Cancel leaves nothing behind.

  • Change a reading: with Add Ø selected, tap its tick. The Edit Ø reading dialog opens with the value; change it and tap Save, or tap Delete.
  • Delete several: choose Remove Ø and tap each tick.

Only one tool is active at a time across the Pits and Diameter measurements sections, so a tap can't place an X and a reading at once. The heading counts the readings, for example 4 recorded.

Measured diameters print as the values you typed, with no Ø symbol, to three decimals in inches (5.738″; a whole number prints bare). On the consolidated sheet the same readings print inside the shaft profile at their station instead.

Wear spots on liners

A wear spot is a band along a liner where it is worn. It prints as a shaded band on the liner's detail strip, dimensioned from the liner's edge, and it is where the worn-profile trace dips.

  1. Open the liner and scroll to Wear spots.
  2. Tap Add spot. A card titled Spot 1 appears; the new band starts at the liner's AFT edge and is 1″ long (or the liner's length, if shorter).
  3. Under Measure From:, tap the datum you measured from: AFT SET, FWD SET, Liner AFT or Liner FWD.
  4. Type the distance into the start field. Its label names the datum — Start from liner AFT edge (in), Start from FWD SET (in) and so on.
  5. Type the band's Length (in).
  6. Optionally add Notes (“scored”, “pitted at 6 o'clock”).

With an AFT datum (AFT SET, Liner AFT) the distance locates the band's AFT edge, measured forward. With a FWD datum (FWD SET, Liner FWD) it locates the band's FWD edge, measured aft. Switching the chip only changes how the distance is shown — the band itself does not move — so you can enter it from one datum and check it from another.

Values commit when you leave the field. A band must fit inside the liner; a value that would push it past either end is refused in the field. If the liner is later shortened so that an existing band runs past its end, the card shows Extends past liner end — re-measure. Delete a band with the bin icon on its card.

The worn-profile trace

When a liner has measured diameters inside a wear spot, the liner's surface line on the detail strip dips through those diameters, so the sheet shows the shape of the wear, not just its numbers. The detail view on screen draws the same trace as the printed strip.

Real wear is a few thousandths on a 6″ liner — far too shallow to see at drawing scale — so the dip is drawn deeper than true. Trace depth exaggeration on the Wear Document tab sets how deep the deepest wear on the job draws, as a percentage of the liner radius, from 5% to 25%; shallower wear draws in proportion to it. The default is 25%. Wear that is deeper than the setting keeps its true proportion — the trace is never drawn shallower than the real wear.

  • The slider value is saved with this job.
  • Save as default makes the current value the app-wide default for jobs that have not set their own. The default also lives at Settings → Drawing → Wear depth exaggeration.

The trace is a picture of the wear, exaggerated on purpose. Machine to the printed Ø values — they are always the numbers that were typed.

Dye pen inspection result

Next to Dye pen inspection:, tap Pass or Fail. The sheet prints an X in that box on its bottom line; the other box stays blank. Tap the selected chip again to clear it and leave both boxes blank for marking by hand.

Which components get a detail strip

By default every liner gets a broken-out detail strip on the printed wear document. To give a taper or body its own strip (so its pits and readings print large), tick it under Components on the Wear Document tab. Default (all liners), All and None set the list in one tap; Complete shaft and Compact strips change the sheet's layout. The full set of layout options is described in The PDF options panel. You can record pits and diameters on any body, taper or liner whether or not it has a strip; without one, they print on the whole-shaft drawing.

Undercuts

What an undercut section is

An undercut is a span machined below the surface — a weld-repair undercut, a cleanup cut under a liner. You record where it is, how long it is and the diameter it was cut to. The app draws each cut as a stepped section in the profile and prints it on its own zoomed detail view with chained dimensions.

Undercuts are measured along the shaft, not tied to a component: a cut may run under a liner edge or span two components.

The Undercut Drawing tab

  • The overview shows the shaft with every cut stepped into it. Every liner, and every bare-shaft area that holds cuts, is tinted as a tap target, with a badge counting its cuts. Tap a liner or highlighted area to open its zoomed detail view.
  • Add undercut records a 1″ section just forward of the AFT S.E.T. and opens its detail view, ready for the real numbers.
  • Cut depth exaggeration and Recorded undercuts appear once there is at least one cut. Each row of the list reads like “1. 3in from Stern tube liner AFT edge” with “L 6in · Ø 5.625in” under it (Ø shows “—” until measured). Tap a row to open that cut; the bin icon deletes it.
  • Blank draft (write-in) and the Print Undercut Drawing, Preview Undercut Drawing and Export Undercut Drawing PDF buttons.

Record a cut

  1. Tap the liner (or highlighted area) on the overview. The detail view opens, titled with that strip.
  2. Tap Add undercut in this liner (on a bare-shaft area, Add undercut here). A card titled New undercut appears, and the cut is drawn in the first free gap. In a liner the distance starts out measured from Liner AFT; on bare shaft, from the nearer S.E.T.
  3. Under Measure From:, tap the datum you measured from (see below).
  4. Type Distance from … — the label names the datum, for example Distance from AFT S.E.T. (in).
  5. Type Length (in).
  6. Type Measured Ø (in). Leave it empty if the cut is not measured yet; it prints no value until it has one.
  7. Optionally add Notes.
  8. Tap Confirm change on the status pill between the drawing and the cards.

The drawing previews every change as you type, but nothing enters the job until it is confirmed. The pill tells you where you stand:

Saved (green tick)
The card matches what is recorded. Nothing to do.
Confirm change
The card has changes that can be saved. Tap to save them. The ✗ beside it (Discard change) throws the changes away.
A warning and a reason
The changes cannot be saved as they stand — for example Overlaps an adjacent undercut — a cut must sit fully inside or fully clear of another. Fix the numbers, or tap ✗ to discard.

You do not have to tap the pill every time. Swiping to another card, tapping another cut on the drawing, or leaving the detail view saves a card whose changes are valid. If a card's changes cannot be saved, the app asks first — Undercut can't be saved, with the reason — and you choose Keep editing or Discard. Tapping outside the question keeps editing, so a stray tap never loses work.

Swipe left and right through the cards (Undercut 1 of 3, and so on), AFT to FWD, or tap a cut on the drawing to jump to its card. Delete a recorded cut with the bin icon on its card. Pinch to zoom the drawing for a closer look.

Measure From: the four datums

ChipThe distance runs…
AFT S.E.T.forward from the AFT S.E.T. to the cut's AFT end
FWD S.E.T.aft from the FWD S.E.T. to the cut's FWD end
Liner AFTforward from the liner's AFT edge to the cut's AFT end
Liner FWDaft from the liner's FWD edge to the cut's FWD end

The liner chips appear only when the cut has a liner to measure from. Two rules keep the cut where you measured it:

  • Changing the datum never moves the cut. The Distance field re-shows the same cut measured from the new datum. Use it to cross-check a measurement from the other end.
  • Changing the length keeps the measured end fixed. The distance you typed stays as typed. Measured from an AFT datum, the cut grows or shrinks at its FWD end; measured from a FWD datum, its FWD end stays put and it grows or shrinks toward AFT.

A cut must lie on the shaft: a distance or length that would put it past either end of the shaft is refused in the field (Undercut extends past the shaft's FWD end, Length must be greater than zero). If the shaft is shortened after a cut is recorded, the cut's card and list row show Extends past shaft end — re-measure.

Nested cuts and overlaps

A nested cut is a smaller, deeper cut inside a wider relief — the shop deepening a corroded patch of an earlier undercut. Record it as its own cut whose span sits entirely inside the wider one; it may run right up to the wider cut's end. The drawing shows it as a second step below the relief's floor, and the dimensions add a row for it.

A cut that sits partly inside another, or exactly on top of one, is blocked with Overlaps an adjacent undercut — a cut must sit fully inside or fully clear of another. Move it fully inside or fully clear of its neighbor.

Two warnings flag a diameter that looks wrong without changing it: Ø meets or exceeds shaft surface here (a cut no smaller than the surface it was cut into), and for a nested cut, Ø meets or exceeds the surrounding cut's floor here.

Cut depth exaggeration

A real undercut is hairline-thin at drawing scale, so its depth is drawn deeper than true. Cut depth exaggeration on the Undercut Drawing tab sets how deep the job's deepest cut draws, from 0% (true scale) to 25%; shallower cuts scale to it, and a cut is never drawn shallower than true. The default is 25%. The setting is saved with the job and changes only the drawing — the printed Ø values are always the numbers you typed.

Line art (no shading)

Two separate switches strip the grey shading from undercut drawings: Line art (no shading) under Settings → Editor Screen → Preview Colors for the screen (see Preview Colors), and Undercut drawing: line art (no shading) for the printed sheet, in Settings → PDF Export Options and the undercut preview's options.

Worn sections (Consolidated Output tab)

A worn section marks a measured area of the shaft on the consolidated sheet. It prints as boundary lines at each end of the area, with the Ø values you took written inside the shaft profile, turned on end, each on a white patch so no drawing line runs through a number.

Add a worn section

  1. Open the Consolidated Output tab and scroll to Worn sections.
  2. Tap Add worn section. The Add worn section dialog opens.
  3. Under Measure from, tap AFT S.E.T. or FWD S.E.T.
  4. Type the Distance (in) from that S.E.T. to the near end of the area, and its Length (in).
  5. Under Measured diameters, type the readings into Ø 1, Ø 2, Ø 3, in order AFT to FWD. Tap Add another Ø for more, up to six.
  6. Tap Save. It is greyed out until there is a distance and a length above zero.

Empty Ø fields are dropped when you save, so you can record the area first and come back with the numbers. The list shows each area as Worn section 1 with a summary such as “12in from AFT S.E.T. · L 8in · 3 Ø” (or “no readings yet”). Tap a row to edit it; the edit dialog adds Delete. As with undercuts, switching the S.E.T. chip re-shows the same area from the other datum without moving it.

Worn sections print only on a consolidated sheet that carries wear information — Sheet content set to All three or Schematic + Wear. They do not appear on the Runout Sheet tab's preview, which shows runouts only.

Working from paper

Print blank drafts

When the tablet can't go to the lathe, print a blank draft — the real drawing of this shaft with the values left empty for handwriting. Turn on Blank draft (write-in) on the Runout Sheet, Wear Document, Undercut Drawing or Consolidated Output tab, then print as usual. For the finished shaft, Print final runout sheet in the banner menu on the Final Schematic tab prints a blank runout sheet marked Final. What each blank draft leaves empty is listed in Blank drafts and Template mode, and how to fill one in is shown in Blank drafts.

Fill it in by hand

  • Write each TIR in its bubble and mark the high spot as a short dash on the rim at its clock position, with the keyway notch at 12 — the same mark the app prints.
  • Write AFT or FORWARD on the TIR line.
  • On the wear form, draw an X for each pit (small or large), hatch the worn bands, write measured diameters beside a tick at the station, and note the distance of each from a datum you will remember — a liner edge or an S.E.T.
  • For undercuts, write distance, datum, length and Ø for each cut.
  • Bubbles are laid out by the shaft's station counts. If you add a station on the shaft, add it on paper between the bubbles it falls between, and add it in the app the same way later.

Type the values back in

  1. Open the job and the matching tab.
  2. Runout: tap each bubble in turn and enter the TIR and high spot; set the TIR orientation. If a station on paper sits away from its printed spot, drag the bubble to match.
  3. Wear: open each component, place the X marks, add the Ø readings and wear spots, set the dye pen result.
  4. Undercuts and worn sections: enter each one with the datum you measured from — the app keeps your numbers as typed, so there is no need to convert to another datum first.
  5. Preview the sheet against the paper copy, then tap Save.

A suggested routine for a repair job

  1. Incoming runout. With the shaft set up, take runouts on every station and the coupling pilot, straight into the Runout Sheet tab or onto a blank runout draft. Set the TIR orientation. Print the runout sheet for the job folder.
  2. Wear survey. Clean up, dye the liners and journals, and record wear spots, pits and measured diameters on the Wear Document tab. Set the dye pen PASS/FAIL. Add worn sections on the Consolidated Output tab for areas you want shown with their Ø values on the consolidated sheet.
  3. Undercut survey. As areas are machined below surface, record each cut on the Undercut Drawing tab — distance, length, measured Ø — and print the undercut record for the welder or the machinist who builds back up.
  4. Repair. Do the work. The original drawing and every reading stay untouched as the record of how the shaft came in.
  5. Final drawing. In the office, the shaft as it leaves — a liner moved, lengthened or shortened — is drawn on the Final Schematic tab (see The Final Schematic tab). It is a separate drawing; nothing on it changes the original or its readings.
  6. Final runout. From the banner menu on the Final Schematic tab, print the blank final runout sheet (Print final runout sheet), take the finished shaft's runouts on it, and file it with the incoming sheet as the before and the after.
